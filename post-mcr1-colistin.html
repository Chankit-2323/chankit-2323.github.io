Plasmid-Mediated Colistin Resistance (mcr-1) and the 2019 Livestock Growth Promotion Ban

Category: One Health Agricultural Policy | Author: Chankit Giri

Colistin (Polymyxin E) represents a crucial last-line option to fight polymyxin-susceptible Gram-negative pan-drug resistant pathogens. For decades, colistin resistance was thought to only occur via slow chromosome mutations (such as mgrB or pmrAB). However, the discovery of the mobile genetic element mcr-1 changed this paradigm. The gene codes for a phosphoethanolamine transferase enzyme that structurally modifies lipopolysaccharides (LPS) on the outer membrane of bacteria, rendering the positive-charge draw of colistin ineffective.

The Non-Therapeutic Agricultural Catalyst & The 2019 Mandate

The global rise of mcr-1 was directly driven by the food animal industry, which used thousands of metric tons of colistin as an off-label growth promoter and prophylactic agent in poultry and swine farming. In response to mounting data, the **Ministry of Health and Family Welfare of India** issued a landmark directive in July 2019 banning the manufacture, sale, and distribution of colistin and its formulations for food-producing animals, poultry, and aquaculture. This vital regulatory boundary was designed to preserve colistin efficacy for critical human infections.
