The Indian Regulatory Timeline on AMR: Evolution of National Action Plans

Category: Public Health Policy | Author: Chankit Giri

India bears one of the highest clinical burdens of AMR globally. Over the last decade, the regulatory landscape has adapted to build a structured defense against resistance selection vectors. This timeline highlights the primary national policy framework milestones:

2011: The Jaipur Declaration
WHO South-East Asia Region Health Ministers signed a commitment to preserve antibiotic efficacy, catalyzing India's internal national policy formulation.
2014: Schedule H1 Implementation
The Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (CDSCO) introduced Schedule H1 to restrict over-the-counter sales of 46 critical antibiotics, mandating specific registers and distinct red-line packaging indicators.
2017: Release of NAP-AMR (Phase 1)
India launched its comprehensive National Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance (2017–2021), aligning with the global One Health approach to monitor human, animal, and environmental sectors.
2022–Present: State-Level Action Plans (SAP-AMR)
Decentralization efforts led states like Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, and Delhi to launch distinct localized stewardship frameworks to target regional hotspots.
